# Patente B Italian–Arabic Dictionary Audit — Batch 24

**Outcome:** Three contextual glosses were corrected using their exact app contexts and cited lexical evidence. No question text or answer key changed. The entries `transitino`, `abitato`/`abitati`, and `delineatore` remain flagged where the evidence does not establish a safe, sufficiently broad Arabic equivalent.

## Three contextual corrections

- **`ripartire` → `يستأنف السير`** — Five exact lemma-token occurrences: three questions (Q20976, Q20790, Q24009) and two lesson-text tokens in `u6-l2`. All five uses concern a vehicle or road user setting off or resuming movement. [Reverso Italian–Arabic](https://dizionario.reverso.net/italiano-arabo/ripartire) lists `الانطلاق`; [Reverso Italian–English examples](https://context.reverso.net/translation/italian-english/di+ripartire) support “to start again/to leave again”; [Al-Maany’s “restarted” entry](https://www.almaany.com/en/dict/ar-en/restarted/) includes resumption senses. The chosen Arabic is context-specific; it is a project-reviewed correction, not owner-approved wording.
- **`avverte` → `يُنَبِّهُ`** — Four exact tokens: three questions (Q21791, Q19031, Q19048) and one lesson-text token in `u2-l2`. The app uses are active warnings/alerts, including about potholes or railway barriers. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/avvertire/) gives the relevant sense of drawing attention, informing, or warning; [Reverso Italian–Arabic](https://dizionario.reverso.net/italiano-arabo/avvertire) supports warning/alert uses and lists `تنبيه`. The active verb better fits these contexts than the former noun draft `تحذير`.
- **`indifferentemente` → `دون تفضيل؛ بحسب السياق: على حدّ سواء`** — Three question tokens (Q23122, Q18319, Q23535), no lesson occurrences. Here the meaning is without distinction or preference, not emotional indifference. [De Mauro/Internazionale](https://dizionario.internazionale.it/parola/indifferentemente) defines the relevant sense as “senza differenza, allo stesso modo”; [Collins](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/us/dictionary/italian-english/indifferentemente) supports “without distinction” and examples where either choice would do. [Reverso’s Italian–Arabic contexts](https://context.reverso.net/traduzione/italiano-arabo/indifferentemente) also show a separate affective sense, which is not appropriate here.

The official [Italian Ministry question corpus](https://www.ilportaledellautomobilista.it/documents/56611/57319/210926_Conseguimento+A-B+italiano.pdf/07091bb8-a16b-4fa1-b921-4fd21fa5b650) was used as contextual reference. Regression tests pin the exact question/lesson IDs, source provenance, and unchanged question-bank hashes.

## Terms left open

- **`abitato` / `abitati`** remain flagged with the existing machine-draft gloss `مأهول`. The relevant [UNECE Arabic PDF for the Convention on Road Traffic](https://unece.org/DAM/trans/conventn/Conv_road_traffic_AR.pdf) could not be read in Arabic here: the web extraction returned PDF bytes and direct download was blocked. The earlier trail had also referred to the separate Arabic [Convention on Road Signs and Signals PDF](https://unece.org/DAM/trans/conventn/Conv_road_signs_2006v_AR.pdf); that is not the appropriate source for the Road Traffic Convention’s Article 1 wording. [Oman’s ratification page](https://qanoon.om/p/2020/rd2020020) renders the relevant definition in English, not Arabic. Separately, [ACI Article 3](https://aci.gov.it/codice-della-strada/art-3/) defines Italy’s *centro abitato* as a sign-delimited statutory category with at least 25 buildings and public-use areas with road access. International “built-up area” wording alone would not establish an exact Arabic equivalent for this Italian legal concept.
- **`delineatore`** remains flagged with the existing draft `المُعَيِّن`. The official-hosted Arabic counterpart to Saudi Highway Code SHC 602 uses **`محددات الطريق`** in section 4-7 / code L33-1 for reflective roadside devices indicating road alignment; its English counterpart describes the same narrower roadside guidance-device category. The [Arabic manual](https://istitlaa.scbc.gov.sa/ar/transportation/mot/saudihighwaycode/Documents/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%84%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%AF%20%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%87%D8%B2%D8%A9%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A%20602.pdf) was checked directly from the public PDF. This supports a generic roadside-delineator expression, but not all Italian statutory subtypes or all 57 exact question contexts. Italy’s [DPR 495/1992, Article 173](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:presidente.repubblica:decreto:1992-12-16;495~art173) and [Article 174](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:presidente.repubblica:decreto:1992-12-16;495~art174) cover normal, tunnel, mountain-road, sharp-curve/hairpin, intersection, access, luminous, temporary, and other delineator uses. Until evidence covers that range or the glossary becomes explicitly context-specific, the broader entry remains unresolved.
- **`transitino`** remains flagged with the machine draft `نقل`. Q23050 and Q31020 use it for vehicles passing; Q18485 uses it for vehicles travelling at a minimum speed. The paired Arabic uses different verbs for passing and travelling, so one unqualified Arabic verb has not been established across all three contexts.

### Clarification of the batch-23 source trail

Batch 23 incorrectly characterized the SHC 602 material as English-only. The government-hosted English PDF has an Arabic counterpart, now checked in parallel; it does provide the specific term `محددات الطريق` for SHC roadside guidance delineators, but its scope is narrower than the Italian legal category and app contexts, so the review flag is retained. The treaty-source trail also needs the distinction above: the Arabic Road Signs and Signals convention is separate from the Arabic Road Traffic convention. Neither the inaccessible Road Traffic PDF nor Oman’s English rendering verifies an Arabic Article 1 equivalent for `abitato`.
